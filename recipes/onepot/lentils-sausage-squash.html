Lentils with sausages, squash and carrots

April 4, 2026  ·  Dutch oven  ·  serves 2  ·  ★★★★★
breakfast sausages dry lentils ~150–180g green squash + yellow squash carrots red onion garlic — 3 cloves bell peppers chicken broth ~400ml white wine ~60ml smoked paprika · coriander · turmeric · red pepper flakes
Before you start
Prep everything first
Red onion — half, diced medium. Garlic — 3 cloves, sliced thin. Carrots — chunky coins or half-moons ~1cm thick. Squash — chunky pieces 2–3cm, skin on is fine. Bell peppers — roughly chopped, similar size to squash. Lentils — 150–180g dry, rinse quickly. Sausages — slice into thick rounds for faster cooking and better browning if not fully defrosted.
Step 1 — 5 min
Brown the sausages
Medium-high heat, Dutch oven, small splash of oil. Sausages in — don't touch for 2 minutes, let them get color. Turn, another 2 minutes. They don't need to be cooked through — just golden outside. Take out, set aside. Leave all the fat in the pot.
Step 2 — 5 min
Build the base
Same pot, medium heat. Onion in — soften 3–4 minutes. Garlic in — 1 minute. Now the spices: 1 tsp smoked paprika, ½ tsp coriander, ½ tsp turmeric, pinch of crushed red pepper flakes, salt and pepper. Stir 30 seconds — they bloom in the fat and wake up completely. It will smell wonderful immediately. This quantity is not bold — it is correct. 😄
Step 3 — simmer 15 min
Lentils and carrots in
Add carrots, stir to coat in spices. Add rinsed lentils, stir. Pour over 400ml chicken broth + 60ml white wine. Liquid should just cover everything — add a splash more broth if needed. Nestle sausages back in. Bring to a boil, then immediately down to a gentle simmer. Cover, leave it. Timer 15 minutes.
Step 4 — simmer 12–15 min
Squash and peppers in
Check lentils — starting to soften but still a little bite. Add squash and bell peppers, push gently into the liquid. Lid back on. Simmer another 12–15 minutes until lentils are fully tender and squash is soft but not collapsed. Check liquid — if too dry add a small splash of broth. It should be saucy but not soupy.
Why stagger the vegetables? Carrots are robust — they go the distance with lentils. Squash and peppers are delicate — they collapse if cooked too long. Adding them last means everything finishes at the right texture at the same time. This is the key move.
Finish
Serve
Off heat. Drizzle of good olive oil. Optional squeeze of lemon to brighten. Serve straight from the Dutch oven into deep bowls. Bread alongside for the broth at the bottom.
Sunny cold April day. Cava Brut alongside. Best sausage-lentil dish ever made in this kitchen — the spice quantities made all the difference. 🥂
This is exactly what the Dutch oven was made for. One pot, zero regrets.
