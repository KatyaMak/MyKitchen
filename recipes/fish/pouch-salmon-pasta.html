Salmon with cherry tomatoes, capers & parsley

April 10, 2026  ·  stovetop  ·  20 minutes  ·  serves 2  ·  ★★★★★
pasta 160–180g (penne or fusilli ideal) salmon pouch — Chicken of the Sea wild Alaskan pink cherry tomatoes ~200–250g, halved garlic — 3 cloves, sliced thin olive oil — generous capers 1 tbsp — or Calabrian peppers substitute ✦ crushed red pepper flakes fresh parsley — generous lemon — half
Start here
Pasta water first — everything else follows
Big pot, generous salt — it should taste like mild seawater. Bring to a full boil. Cook 160–180g pasta until just al dente — 1–2 minutes less than the package says. It finishes in the sauce. Before draining, reserve a full cup of pasta water — don't skip this.
Step 1 — while pasta cooks
The garlic base
Wide pan, medium heat. About 3 tablespoons olive oil. Slice 3 garlic cloves thin — sliced, not crushed, so they melt into the sauce. Gently golden, 2–3 minutes. Not brown. Add a pinch of crushed red pepper flakes.
Step 2 — 5–6 min
Blister the cherry tomatoes
About 200–250g cherry tomatoes, halved. Medium-high heat. Let them sit without stirring for a minute, then toss. You want them to blister, collapse, and go jammy. They should look like a loose sauce. Salt and pepper.
Step 3 — two options
The briny element
Classic: Add 1 tablespoon capers, stir in. They pop against the sweet tomato — that contrast is the whole point.

Substitute: A small spoonful of chopped Calabrian peppers from the fridge. Different character — more heat, less brine — but equally good with salmon and tomatoes. Or a few sliced jalapeños. Both work. Note which you used — they're genuinely different directions. 😄
Step 4 — 60 seconds maximum
Salmon — gentle, last
Open the pouch, flake roughly into the pan — chunky pieces, not mashed. Gentle heat only — the salmon is already cooked, you are just warming it through. 60 seconds maximum. Done. Overworking it makes it dry and sad.
Finish
Bring it together
Drained pasta straight into the pan. Toss well. Add pasta water gradually — a few splashes at a time — until the sauce coats everything and looks glossy. Squeeze of half a lemon. Generous fresh parsley — be really generous, it lifts everything. Raw drizzle of good olive oil. Toss once more. Plate immediately.

Parmesan? Traditionally no. But a very light grating of Boar's Head at the end is acceptable if you want it. Don't stir it in. 😄
Sunny April evening. Light, bright, Mediterranean. Fast enough for a weeknight, good enough to feel like a real dinner. 🍝🌞
