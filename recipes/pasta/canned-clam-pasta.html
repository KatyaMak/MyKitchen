Pasta alle vongole — canned clam version

April 3, 2026  ·  stovetop  ·  20 minutes  ·  serves 2  ·  ★★★★★
pasta 160–180g (linguine classic — any long or mixed works) chopped clams × 2 cans — keep the brine! garlic — 3–4 cloves, sliced thin olive oil — generous white wine ~60ml crushed red pepper flakes Sicilian spice mix or parsley lemon
Start here
Pasta water first — everything else is fast
Big pot, generously salted. Cook pasta until just al dente — 1–2 minutes less than package says. It finishes in the sauce. Before draining, reserve a large cup of pasta water — this is important, don't forget it.
Step 1 — while pasta cooks
The garlic base
Wide pan, medium heat, generous olive oil. Slice 3–4 garlic cloves thin — sliced, not crushed, so they melt into the sauce. Gently golden, not brown. Add a good pinch of crushed red pepper flakes. Do not rush this step.
Step 2 — liquid gold
The clam brine
Open clam cans. Drain clams but keep the brine in a separate cup — this is your flavour base, never discard it. Add brine to the pan first, let it bubble and reduce 1–2 minutes. Add ~60ml white wine, reduce another minute. The brine carries salt already — taste before adding any more.
Step 3 — 30 seconds maximum
The clams
Add clams to the pan. Gentle heat — they are already cooked, you are just warming them. 30 seconds maximum. Done. Don't overcook or they go rubbery.
Finish
Bring it together
Add drained pasta straight into the clam pan. Toss well. Add pasta water gradually — a few splashes at a time — until the sauce coats everything and looks glossy, not dry, not soupy. This glossy moment is the whole point.

Squeeze of lemon. Generous Sicilian spice mix or dry parsley — be generous with herbs, they can take it. Raw drizzle of good olive oil at the very end. Toss once more. Plate immediately — don't let it sit.
No parmesan — seafood pasta, Italians would be horrified. 😄
Garlic stays in — it melts completely into the sauce.
More garlic than you think — 3–4 minimum, never 1.
The brine is everything — it's why canned clams work so well.
"Italian happiness." Turned out this good from canned clams — genuinely surprising. 🍝🥂
