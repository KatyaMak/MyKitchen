Caprese pasta

April 2026  ·  stovetop  ·  20 minutes  ·  serves 1–2  ·  ★★★★★
fusilli 160–180g cherry tomatoes ~200g, halved fresh mozzarella — torn by hand fresh basil — generous fresh oregano fresh thyme — a few leaves garlic — 2 cloves, sliced thin Calabrian peppers — small spoonful olive oil — generous, twice lemon — small squeeze
Start here
Pasta water first
Big pot, well salted. Bring to a full boil. Cook fusilli until just al dente — 1–2 minutes less than the package says. It finishes with the tomatoes. Before draining, reserve a cup of pasta water.
Step 1 — while pasta cooks
The garlic base
Wide pan, medium heat, generous olive oil. Slice garlic thin — sliced, not crushed, so it melts into the oil. Gently golden, 2–3 minutes. Not brown. Add a small spoonful of Calabrian peppers — little heat, little depth.
Step 2 — 3–4 min
Tomatoes — just warm them
Cherry tomatoes halved, into the pan. Medium heat — you're not blistering them here, just warming them through so they release their juices and soften slightly. 3–4 minutes. Salt lightly. This is a fresh dish, not a cooked sauce — keep the heat gentle.
Step 3
Fresh herbs in
Fresh oregano leaves stripped directly in — generous. A few fresh thyme leaves. Stir once. Off heat.

Sage — not here. Sage is warm and earthy and fights the bright Caprese direction. Save it for butter pasta.
Finish
Bring it together
Drained pasta into the pan. Toss well. Add pasta water in small splashes until everything looks glossy and loose — fusilli drinks it fast, be generous. Small squeeze of lemon. Raw drizzle of good olive oil. Toss once more.

Mozzarella — off heat, last. Tear by hand directly over the pasta — chunky irregular pieces. Let it sit 30 seconds so it just softens slightly rather than melts completely. That's the move.

Fresh basil torn over the top. Plate immediately.
Solo Saturday dinner. Cherry tomatoes, fresh mozzarella, Cava alongside. Light, fresh, barely cooked — summer on a plate in April. 🍝🥂
